Listing what has already been checked or ruled out by others

Someone else may already have looked at part of your system: a heating technician at the furnace, an appliance technician at the dryer, or an earlier duct provider. You may know who checked what and whether they said it or wrote it, but only roughly when. In your Prince Edward County request, write down what you know, keep it apart from guesses, and mark anything passed on second-hand as such. This lets a responding provider see which questions remain open and which statements still need confirming before shaping the scope.

  1. Return grilles

    Where room air is drawn back toward the furnace.

  2. Return ducts

    The runs leading from each grille to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    The blower, evaporator coil and cabinet.

  4. Supply trunk

    The largest duct carrying air away from the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    Smaller ducts feeding each room.

  6. Supply registers

    Where air comes back into each room.

Separate runs to ask about:Dryer ventHRV core and screensBathroom fan ductsRange hood exhaust

  • Who looked, and when

    Name each person or company that checked part of the system and note the approximate date of each visit.

  • What was said or written

    Mark each finding as written, spoken or passed on second-hand, and attach any invoice or note you hold.

  • What remains open

    List the parts nobody mentioned and the questions still unanswered, so the provider knows where to start.

Questions About Your Prince Edward County Request

Is Prince Edward County Duct Cleaning a duct contractor, and did it make the earlier visits?

It has no staff of its own and no vehicles, and it carried out none of the earlier visits to your home, so this site is an enquiry website for Prince Edward County, not a contractor. Each request goes to our enquiry team for review and may then go to a provider. Any provider it reaches must identify themselves and confirm coverage of your address before anything is arranged.

What happens to my Prince Edward County request after I send it?

You and a provider who may respond later are the ones to agree a written scope with its timing and pricing, and nothing has been arranged on your behalf until that happens; a verbal assurance given by phone or at the door is not part of it. Sending a request gives our enquiry team your description and any notes about earlier checks. It does not book anyone, confirm coverage, set a price or fix a date.

Which earlier technicians should I mention when I send my request?

Mention anyone who looked at any part of the system: a heating technician, an appliance technician, a ventilation contractor or an earlier duct provider. For each, add the approximate date, what they examined and whether they said it or wrote it. Missing details are fine; say which are unknown, and the provider will treat those parts as still open.

How do I mark something a technician told me but never wrote down?

Label it as spoken and second-hand, with the approximate date and the person who said it. The responding provider treats it as a lead to confirm, not as an established finding, and may ask you to check whether the earlier party can supply a written note. Keep the wording as close to what was said as you can recall.

Can one Prince Edward County request cover both the ducts and the dryer vent?

One request can describe both. The dryer exhaust and the furnace ducts are separate systems, so list what was checked on each, by whom and when, and the provider can scope the two separately and note which earlier remarks apply to which. Photographs of the connector, the outside hood and the registers help to keep the two apart.

What if the earlier invoice does not say which parts were checked?

Send it anyway and note what is unclear. An invoice that says service completed without naming components leaves those parts open. The provider may ask you to approach the earlier party for details, and any part without a record stays on the list of open questions until someone has looked at it and written down what they found.

Who decides what work happens in my Prince Edward County property after I send this?

You and the responding provider agree the written scope. Where a landlord, property manager or condominium corporation controls the system, that party must authorize the work. Our enquiry team does not decide scope, and an earlier technician's comment does not settle it either, because the provider confirms what applies to the system actually in the home.

Should I tell the provider what I think an earlier technician got wrong?

Stick to what was said, what was written and when. An opinion that someone made a mistake is not needed and cannot be confirmed from here. The provider forms a view from the system itself and notes where it differs from, or cannot verify, an earlier statement, so your list of facts is more useful than a judgement about anyone's work.
